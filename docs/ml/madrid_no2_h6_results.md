# Madrid NO2 at t+6 h: ExFuzzy versus baselines on a chronological split (AT-041)

Real data: the `madrid_no2_h6` table of AT-011 (see `docs/air/madrid_no2_h6.md` for the time convention). Model
family: ex_fuzzy 3.3.1 zero-order TSK (`BaseFuzzyRulesRegressor`, crisp consequents, `additive`, CPU `pymoo`).
Every number below comes from the run artifacts (`metrics.json`, `summary.json`; tables generated by
`python -m atlas.ml.report`). Rules are linguistic over quantile partitions fitted on the training rows only.
**No causal claim is made, firing is a degree of rule match (not a probability, risk or importance), and nothing
here is a clinical, sanitary or regulatory statement.**

## 1. Result in five lines

* HistGradientBoosting wins everywhere: test MAE 10.9 / RMSE 14.9 / R2 0.39 µg/m³ against ExFuzzy 14.0 / 17.8 / 0.13
  (seed 0; 3 seeds: R2 0.116 ± 0.013). It also wins at all 22 test stations.
* ExFuzzy sits at the level of Ridge (MAE 13.8 / RMSE 17.5 / R2 0.16), better than persistence (RMSE 22.9, R2 -0.44) and
  slightly better than the hour x weekday climatology (RMSE 18.5, R2 0.05).
* The gap is not a data-size effect: HGB fit on the same 30,000 rows as ExFuzzy reaches R2 0.36 (MAE 11.1).
* ExFuzzy never predicts a value at or above 100 µg/m³ (event recall 0 of 674 hours); HGB reaches 0.10.
* The rule base is readable but poor: 30 rules, 8 duplicates, several rules with a 1 µg/m³ consequent, and the rule
  sets of the three seeds overlap by only a third (section 6). It was published as the real model for the web, with
  these metrics attached.

### Deviation from the first plan (stated for transparency)

The first attempt used a 100,000-row subsample and 1,600 evaluations per fit (`n_gen` 40 x `pop_size` 40), following
the cost model of `docs/ml/exfuzzy_api_notes.md` §7 that suggested 200 to 400 k rows. Three of its four fits finished
before it was stopped (validation R2 0.123, 0.112, 0.078 for 15x2, 15x3, 30x2; kept in
`experiments/at041-v0-underbudget/selection.json`): the GA was evaluation-starved, not data-starved. A 30,000-row
pilot showed train R2 0.04 after 1,600 evaluations and 0.17 after 7,200, so the evaluation count matters more than
the row count and the per-row cost is also lower for small sets (0.8 µs per row and evaluation at 30 k rows against
2.5 µs at 100 k). The final design therefore trades rows for evaluations (30 k rows, 20,000 evaluations). The test
period was not used to take this decision. The recommendation of section 7 of the API notes is superseded for this
task.

Reading:

* R2 is low for every model because a 6 h horizon crosses the daily cycle and because the test year is cleaner than
  the training period. Every model except persistence **over-predicts** (positive bias of 3.6 to 6.9 µg/m³): the
  training period is 7 µg/m³ higher on average (the downward trend noted in the AT-011 documentation). The
  climatology bias (6.8) is almost exactly the level difference. Nothing was corrected with test information.
* Persistence is unbiased but has the worst RMSE: at +6 h the current value says little (the horizon moves the
  series across the daily peaks).
* ExFuzzy beats persistence at 19 of the 22 stations and the per-station climatology at 18, but beats Ridge at only 7
  (MAE per station) and HGB at none.

### 4.3 Per local hour of the target (test MAE)

ExFuzzy is close to HGB at night and around 20:00 to 23:00 (hours 0, 21 to 23: 15.0 / 15.5 / 16.7 / 16.5 against
14.9 / 15.6 / 16.8 / 16.2) and far from it in the afternoon (hour 16: 15.1 against 7.4) and at 03:00 to 05:00 (about
15 against 8 to 10). It is worse than persistence at hours 17 to 19 (16.5 to 17.0 against 11.8 to 12.7). The model
barely uses the hour: `valid_hour_local` appears in only 4 of 30 rules, while HGB, which sees the hour with its
fine splits, follows the daily curve. The per-month and per-hour tables are in `metrics.json`
(`month=1`..`12`, `hour=0`..`23`) and are reproduced by `python -m atlas.ml.report`.

### 4.4 Extreme episodes (analytical thresholds, not regulatory limits)

The EU hourly limit value (200 µg/m³) is **never** reached in the validation or test periods (0 hours; 122 hours in
training). The thresholds below are descriptive: the training 99th percentile (116 µg/m³; 148 test hours, 0.08 %)
and a fixed 100 µg/m³ (674 test hours, 0.37 %).

| threshold | estimator | events | MAE on events | bias on events | event recall | event precision | alarms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| >= 100 | persistence | 674 | 80.35 | -80.32 | 0.01 | 0.01 | 724 |
| >= 100 | HGB | 674 | 44.78 | -43.66 | 0.10 | 0.27 | 257 |
| >= 100 | ExFuzzy | 674 | 69.28 | -69.28 | 0.00 | n/a (no alarm) | 0 |
| >= 116 (train p99) | persistence | 148 | 93.82 | -93.82 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 161 |
| >= 116 (train p99) | HGB | 148 | 55.33 | -54.12 | 0.10 | 0.20 | 75 |
| >= 116 (train p99) | ExFuzzy | 148 | 82.51 | -82.51 | 0.00 | n/a (no alarm) | 0 |

Recall is the share of the event hours predicted at or above the level; "alarm" is a prediction at or above the
level. No estimator, HGB included, anticipates these hours with any practical reliability; the fuzzy model's range is
bounded by its consequents and its predictions on those hours are the mean of lower consequents. Event counts are
small (148, 674): treat these rows as descriptive, not as skill estimates.

are cheap and its artifact is tiny. That asymmetry is the cost of the GA, not of the TSK inference.

## 6. Interpretability

Counts (test rows; seed 0 / 1 / 2):

| | seed 0 | seed 1 | seed 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| rules | 30 | 30 | 30 |
| mean rule length | 1.63 | 1.50 | 1.70 |
| duplicate rules (same antecedents as an earlier rule) | 8 | 11 | 8 |
| distinct antecedent sets | 22 | 19 | 22 |
| fraction of test rows using the fallback (`y_mean`) | 0.0001 | 0.0103 | 0.0045 |

* **Stability across seeds**: pairwise Jaccard index of the sets of distinct antecedent sets 0.33 to 0.37 (mean
  0.356); 68 %, 79 % and 68 % of each model's antecedent sets appear in at least one other seed (mostly the
  one-antecedent rules). Share of rules that mention a feature, seed 0 / 1 / 2: `no2_last` 0.40 / 0.53 / 0.57,
  `no2_lag3h` 0.43 / 0.40 / 0.33, `wind_speed` 0.27 / 0.13 / 0.23, `no2_lag1h` 0.13 / 0.13 / 0.20, `valid_hour_local`
  0.13 / 0.03 / 0.13, `temperature` 0.13 / 0.07 / 0.07, `relative_humidity` 0.07 / 0.03 / 0.10, `no2_lag24h` 0.07 / 0.13 /
  0.07, `valid_weekday` 0 / 0.03 / 0. This is how often a feature is *named*, not how much it matters. The
  same inputs (last and 3-hour-old NO2, wind) recur, but the rule sets as wholes are not reproducible, and the
  numeric consequents differ more.
* **The rules are not individually reliable statements.** In `additive` mode the prediction is the firing-weighted
  mean of all consequents (docs/ml/rule_contribution.md), and the GA places consequents on its own grid, so
  neighbouring rules compensate each other. Example (seed 0): `no2_last IS Low` appears in R13 (51.2 µg/m³), R26 (8.5)
  and R27 (1.0), which act as one rule with the mean consequent (20.2); R08, R18 and R24 (`no2_lag3h IS Low`) are 6.0, 1.0
  and 38.7. Reading R13 alone ("when the last NO2 is low, expect 51") would be wrong. A canonical, duplicate-merged
  view would be needed before showing rules as sentences (proposal in section 9).
* Activation (`mean_activation`, `activation_frequency` at tau 0.5, test): one-antecedent rules fire on about 40 %
  of the rows because a quantile term covers about a third of the data by construction; two-antecedent rules fire on
  7 to 27 %.
* The partitions are fixed before the GA: 3 trapezoids per feature at the quantiles of the 30,000-row training
  subsample (table in section 6.2). `Low` of NO2 means up to 10 to 17 µg/m³, `Medium` 10 to 47, `High` 28 or more.
  Inputs outside the training range take the membership of the nearest edge (library behaviour).

## 7. Why the fuzzy model loses (what is and is not established)

Established by these runs: HGB, on the same inputs and on the same 30,000 rows, is 2.6 (test) to 2.9 (validation) µg/m³ better in RMSE and
0.23 (test) better in R2; so the loss is not the subsample. The pilot and the first attempt show that the GA objective
(train R2) rises steeply with the number of evaluations at the start (budget check below).

Budget check (`configs/ml/madrid_no2_h6_budget_check.json`, experiment `at041-budget4x`, 15 rules x 2 antecedents, seed
0, same 30,000 rows): 1,000 generations x 80 (80,000 evaluations, 628 s) returned **exactly** the model of the
250-generation fit (identical validation MAE / RMSE / R2 to every printed digit). Between 1,600 and 7,200 evaluations
the train R2 of the 30x3 pilot went from 0.04 to 0.17, but beyond about 20,000 the GA had stagnated for this shape:
four times the evaluations did not move the rule base. A larger budget of the same GA is therefore not the obvious fix
(not tested for the 30x2 shape that was published).

Plausible but **not tested** here: (a) three trapezoids per feature and crisp consequents cannot represent the sharp
hour-of-day structure, and `valid_hour_local` as a raw 0 to 23 number puts 23 h and 0 h far apart (AT-011 note);
`valid_hour_sin/cos` or `valid_is_weekend` were not tried because the A1 recommendation was the nine features; (b)
the GA is weak at this problem size (random initial rule bases, 20,000 evaluations, 30 rules x up to 2 antecedents).

## 9. Limitations, risks and proposals

* Single chronological split and one test year, trending down; no confidence intervals beyond the 3-seed spread.
  Errors are strongly autocorrelated in time and across stations, so the per-station and per-month rows are not
  independent samples.
* Meteorology comes from a different place than the station (up to 4.6 km, never interpolated); 16 % of rows use a
  substitute meteorological station. Station 28079058 has no wind and no complete row; 28079055 has no NO2 in 2025.
* `complete` rows only: the published activation tables cover all 2025 issue hours, with null rows (not zeros) where an
  input is missing (3 of 24 stations at the sample hour used in the smoke test).
* The published model is the weakest estimator of the study that is not a trivial baseline, yet it is the one the web
  will show. The model card has the metrics; any UI text must not present its predictions as reliable or its rules as
  explanations of NO2 behaviour. Firing strength is not a probability, not a risk and not a contribution.
* Nothing is claimed about real-time use: publication latency of the portal was not measured, and the 2025 values are
  a retrospective replay.
* Proposals: (1) a canonical rule view that groups duplicates and shows the weighted mean consequent, before rules are
  shown as sentences; (2) a follow-up with other GA settings (restarts, a seeded initial population) rather than a larger budget of the
  same GA (section 7), and, separately, a feature variant with cyclic hour / weekend (a feature-owner decision, A1);
  (3) `rules.json` activation summaries do not state their reference period, and `metrics.json` has no period field
  (the `validation/` prefix is a convention introduced here); a `period` field in `MetricRow` would be cleaner;
  (4) an upstream ex_fuzzy option to seed the GA or to evaluate on row chunks would help more than more rows.
